# Albert's MTG Deckbuilding Philosophy & Profile
# Last updated: 2026-05-04

---

## Who I Am as a Player

Albert is a **collector-first, builder-second** Magic player who is actively growing into the strategic side of the game. He tracks his physical collection carefully, cares about card condition, and approaches deckbuilding with genuine enthusiasm and curiosity. He is getting better at politicking at the table and enjoys the social dynamics of Commander.

He is **budget-conscious but ambitious** — he wants powerful, impactful cards but weighs price vs. hate-magnet risk carefully. He prefers to build towards something great over time rather than overspend impulsively.

---

## Primary Deck: Mono-Black Sacrifice & Reanimation

**Commander:** Sheoldred, Whispering One *(preferred)* / Sheoldred (March of the Machine) *(include)*
**Format:** Commander (EDH)
**Power Level Target:** Mid-power — enhanced precon level, NOT cEDH

### Core Philosophy
> "I want a slower game that builds towards inevitable defeat. I want control of the board and for them to have to band up to even think about taking me down."

This is an **Inevitability Control** deck. Not a turn-3 combo deck. Not a glass cannon. A slow, oppressive machine that:
- Makes every death benefit Albert (life gain, card draw, triggers)
- Makes every sacrifice cost opponents something too
- Builds board presence quietly until resistance is futile
- Wins with a **grand, definitive moment** — not a cheap instant-win

### What Every Card Should Do
Albert's guiding rule: *everything should happen for a reason.*
- Creature dies for an opponent → Albert gains life
- Albert sacrifices a creature → opponent must sacrifice too
- Albert loses life → there must be a meaningful payoff
- Cards that create value loops, not dead draws

### Playstyle Preferences
- **Likes:** Sacrifice outlets, death triggers, reanimation, token creation, aristocrat-style payoffs, board control, inevitability
- **Loves:** Sheoldred, Whispering One (the deck's identity card — reanimate + opponent sacrifices = pure control)
- **Comfortable with:** Infinite combos as a *late-game closer*, not a primary gameplan. One or two combos is enough.
- **Prefers:** Grander, more theatrical wins — a definitive board state victory over a quiet one

### Threat Assessment & Table Politics
- Albert is **actively developing his politicking skills** — he enjoys the social negotiation aspect of Commander
- He prefers to **fly under the radar** early and become the archenemy only when the time is right
- He plays against smart mid-power players who combo, use counters, and cycle — he respects this but wants to be able to handle it

### Dislikes / Pain Points
- **Graveyard hate** — his deck's engine lives in the graveyard; when it gets exiled he loses his primary resource
- **Stealing from his graveyard** — deeply annoying; Animate Dead on his own creatures is infuriating
- **Counterspells** — he fears them and wants to play around them when possible
- **Board wipes by opponents** — he typically prefers to be the one wiping the board, to great theatrical effect

## Wishlist Philosophy

Sheoldred, the Apocalypse is the canonical example of Albert's wishlist philosophy:
> *"It's really really nice, but its price and the hate it would get in a game makes it a wishlist item."*

A card goes to the wishlist when:
1. It costs significantly more than budget allows right now
2. It would immediately paint a target on his head at the table
3. It's "dream card" territory — great to own someday, not essential now

## Collection Preferences

- Tracks collection in a CSV/spreadsheet (Moxfield-style export format)
- Cares about condition: Near Mint preferred, Lightly Played acceptable, Moderately Played only for budget
- **Foils:** Keeps track of foil status; doesn't chase them but notes them
- Buys from TCGPlayer; price-conscious when building shopping lists
- Does not want to accidentally buy duplicates — always check for duplicates before ordering

---

## Tool & Script Preferences

- Prefers **terminal-based workflows** for now (building toward a web UI later)
- Wants scripts to be **interactive and conversational** — answer a few questions, not memorize flags
- Output files should always be **date-stamped** to avoid duplicate confusion
- Outputs go to `~/Downloads` for easy access
- Likes **clear summaries** at the end of any run — what matched, what didn't, what was saved
